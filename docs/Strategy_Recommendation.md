# Strategy Recommendation

## My recommendation

Based on the synthetic scoring model, I would not try to enter all eight markets at the same time.

The four Tier-1 markets are:

1. **United Kingdom** - score 57.6
2. **Germany** - score 56.8
3. **France** - score 54.1
4. **Netherlands** - score 51.2

## Suggested first focus

I would start the commercial validation work in **United Kingdom and Germany**.

They combine relatively large addressable markets with strong commercial attractiveness, but the reasons are not identical. That is useful because it allows the company to test whether the same offer works in two different market environments.

## Customer focus

The highest-scoring customer segment in the synthetic model is:

**Vehicle OEMs**

I would still keep OEMs in the strategic pipeline, but I would avoid assuming that the largest segment is automatically the easiest first customer. Long qualification cycles can make a smaller but faster-moving segment more useful for an early pilot.

## 12-month approach

### Phase 1 - Validate
- customer interviews
- competitor tracker
- pricing guardrails

### Phase 2 - Pilot
- two customer pilots
- local channel/partner test
- product/certification gap review

### Phase 3 - Scale
- named-account planning
- expand only after pilot and pipeline thresholds are met

## Why I would not start with every attractive market

One thing I learned from this project is that market prioritization should also consider execution capacity.

A small business-development team can easily create a long list of attractive countries. The harder decision is choosing where to spend limited sales, technical and management time.

The scoring model is therefore a way to structure the discussion, not a substitute for customer evidence.
